# Ranked recommendation

## 1. Daily Learning Studio — recommended

This is the best Alpha direction because it closes the highest-impact gap—the dashboard/hub downgrade—while preserving the lesson/activity work that is already strong. It gives beginners an obvious next step, makes the six hubs legible as different tools, completes the current vocabulary-image gap, and adds only a controlled amount of new visual production. The medium-density composition also travels cleanly across 360, 390, 820 and 1440 without requiring atlas-like interaction or console-level compression.

Choose it if the goal is a credible, premium Alpha that feels coherent from the first viewport through lesson, hub, detail and review. Sequence it conceptually as: developer-language cleanup and card anatomy first; dashboard/mission hierarchy second; hub/detail consolidation third; selective assets last. That sequence protects UX value if asset production arrives in batches.

## 2. Compact Learning Console

This is the strongest budget and delivery-risk option. It produces a large usability gain through hierarchy, semantic propagation, responsive density and plain-language cleanup while using the existing 9 PNGs, 3 SVGs and CSS teaching components. It is especially suitable if Alpha success is measured by repeat study efficiency and engineering certainty.

It ranks second because the visual gap to the approved illustration-led composites remains partly open. The HTML/CSS meaning plate is honest and accessible, but eight unillustrated vocabulary details will still feel less memorable than a complete illustrated system. The direction needs careful artless polish—spacing, typography, borders and state cues—to avoid reading as an admin console.

## 3. Illustrated Knowledge Atlas

This is the most distinctive and closest to the approved composite richness, but it is the wrong first commitment for the Alpha. It adds the largest asset queue, the hardest responsive map behavior and the most accessibility equivalents before the basic dashboard/hub hierarchy is normalized. It is a good north-star after the Daily Learning Studio establishes reusable object anatomies and after asset production proves consistent.

## Decision summary

| Rank | Direction | Best for | Claude engineering | Codex assets | Principal tradeoff |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Daily Learning Studio | Balanced premium Alpha | M | M | Requires coordinated card + asset rollout |
| 2 | Compact Learning Console | Fast, lean, repeat-study UX | M | S | Less illustration-led and less memorable |
| 3 | Illustrated Knowledge Atlas | Future flagship richness | L | L | Asset/accessibility/responsive burden |

## Best fit for a $20/month Codex asset budget

**Compact Learning Console** is the clear fit. Its estimated new image-asset count is zero; Codex effort can be spent on a small, reusable inline icon/meaning-plate specification and QA rather than a growing illustration queue. It still reuses every suitable approved PNG/SVG and materially improves the dashboard, hubs, details, practice, review and settings.

If the budget can accumulate across several months, use **Daily Learning Studio** as the product direction but phase its assets: ship the structural uplift first, then produce the eight missing vocabulary visuals in reviewed batches. Do not mix temporary stock imagery or inconsistent generated styles while waiting.

## Final direction guardrails

- Lock the existing semantic tokens and gender color + shape + label rules before any visual work.
- Make learner-language cleanup a release criterion, not polish: internal publication/evidence/game-state language must disappear in all options.
- Validate 360, 390, 820 and 1440 plus keyboard, 200% zoom, AA contrast and reduced motion before considering the direction complete.
- Keep light mode for Alpha. Dark mode is future research, not part of this decision.
- Do not expand rights-gated audio access or publish review-only content as a visual shortcut.
